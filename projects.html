Current Projects

Active research threads at the intersection of computational social science and policy evaluation.

Three Active Threads

  1. 01

    AI · Context · Europe

    AI & Society

    How European publics accept generative AI across assistant, peer, and supervisor roles — and how national media, scholarly, and policy regimes bend the literacy-to-acceptance gradient. The empirical core is a set of mixed-effects models on CRONOS-3 Wave 5 (11 countries, N ≈ 7,700), with country-level moderators built from GDELT 2.0 news (2018–2025), OpenAlex scholarly output, and the OECD AI Observatory’s labour data. Early findings: media coverage compresses the literacy-to-acceptance slope, while scholarly impact amplifies it.

    2025 – ongoing · mixed-effects · cross-level interactions · Oxford Week 1 seminar →

  2. 02

    Occupational Embeddings, 1800s–2000s

    NLP & History

    A diachronic word-embedding pipeline tracing two centuries of occupational gender-typing and cultural prestige through Google Ngram (American English, 5-gram). Decade-specific Word2Vec (SGNS) models with orthogonal-Procrustes alignment to a 2000s baseline, run on Oxford ARC under Slurm. The pipeline extends Jiang et al. (2025) on the cultural devaluation of feminized work, and feeds a ‘spaces’ sub-project on the social geography of kitchens, offices, factories, churches, parks, and prisons — 87 anchor tokens selected via a 5-stage pre-registered vocabulary protocol.

    2024 – ongoing · Word2Vec · Procrustes alignment · HPC (Slurm / ARC)

  3. 03

    Flexible Working & Affective Well-being

    Policy Eval

    A two-decade evaluation of UK flexible-working legislation (2003–2024). Episode-level time-diary models test whether the statutory expansion has delivered well-being gains to the parents and carers it was designed for, and document how the working-from-home uptake curve moves across the COVID-19 period. A central puzzle the project surfaces: flexible-working legislation has continued to strengthen, yet mothers are increasingly less likely to actually work from home.

    2024 – ongoing · UKTUS · episode-level models · sequence analysis · GenTime project
