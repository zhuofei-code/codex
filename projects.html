Current Projects

Active research threads on generative AI, the future of work, and gender.

Three Active Threads

  1. 01

    AI · Context · Europe

    AI & Society

    How European publics accept generative AI across assistant, peer, and supervisor roles — and how national media, scholarly, and policy regimes bend the literacy-to-acceptance gradient. The empirical core is a set of mixed-effects models on CRONOS-3 Wave 5 (11 countries, N ≈ 7,700), with country-level moderators built from GDELT 2.0 news (2018–2025), OpenAlex scholarly output, and the OECD AI Observatory’s labour data. Early findings: media coverage compresses the literacy-to-acceptance slope, while scholarly impact amplifies it.

    2025 – ongoing · mixed-effects · cross-level interactions · John Fell Fund, £10,000 (PI) · working paper under review · Oxford Week 1 seminar →

  2. 02

    Transformers vs. Word2Vec, 1800s–2000s

    NLP & Culture

    Whether transformer architectures recover cultural meaning from historical text better than the static word vectors diachronic research has relied on. BERT-family contextual embeddings are built from full-text historical American English, then compared against the standard pipeline, namely decade-specific Word2Vec (SGNS) models on Google Ngram aligned to a 2000s baseline by orthogonal Procrustes. Both pipelines are evaluated on the same 87 anchor tokens, selected through a 5-stage pre-registered vocabulary protocol. The substantive targets are occupational gender-typing and cultural prestige, extending Jiang et al. (2025) on the cultural devaluation of feminized work, and a ‘spaces’ sub-project on the social geography of kitchens, offices, factories, churches, parks, and prisons.

    2024 – ongoing · BERT-family contextual embeddings · Word2Vec baseline · HPC (Slurm / ARC)

  3. 03

    Flexible Working & Affective Well-being

    Work & Gender

    Who works from home in Britain, and who actually enjoys it. Episode-level time-diary models on the UK Time Use Survey (2003–2024) track how homeworking spread across the COVID-19 period, and compare the affective well-being of mothers, fathers, and workers without children during the hours they spend working at home. The project surfaces a puzzle. Statutory flexible-working rights have kept widening, yet mothers have become less likely to work from home, not more.

    2024 – ongoing · UKTUS · episode-level models · sequence analysis · GenTime project · working paper under review
