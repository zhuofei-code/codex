Zhuofei Lu

Assistant Professor of Sociology (Computational Social Science)
University of Warwick

I joined the University of Warwick in September 2026 as an assistant professor of sociology (computational social science). Before that I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Sociology at the University of Oxford, working on the ‘GenTime’ project and convening quantitative methods courses in the School of Global and Area Studies. My work is in computational social science and covers five connected topics, namely the future of work, generative AI, social stratification, gender and culture, and human-centred AI governance. I draw on survey, time-diary, biomarker, and large text-corpus data. Three threads are active right now. The first is public acceptance of generative AI across eleven European countries, and how national media and scholarly contexts shape that acceptance. The second is two centuries of occupational gender-typing recovered from historical text corpora. The third is who works from home in Britain, and whether the people who do so enjoy it.

Methodologically I work mainly in natural language processing. Transformer architectures and word-embedding models such as Word2Vec let me trace how occupations and social groups are described across large text corpora. Variational autoencoders (i.e., neural models that compress many indicators into a few latent dimensions) let me recover stratification structure from survey data instead of fixing that structure in advance. Alongside this I model large-scale longitudinal household panel and time-diary data. I serve on the editorial boards of Work, Employment & Society and Social Science Research, and as Associate Editor of Humanities and Social Sciences Communications.
